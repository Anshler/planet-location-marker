## Hipparcos, the New Reduction — public data

Parallaxes, their errors and proper motions come from F. van Leeuwen, *Validation of the new
Hipparcos reduction*, A&A 474, 653 (2007), as distributed by NASA's High Energy Astrophysics
Science Archive Research Center (HEASARC table `hipnewcat`,
<https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/star-catalog/hipnewcat.html>). The table is pinned by
SHA-256 in `vendor/VENDOR.json` (a gitignored build input); `scripts/build-stars.mjs` extracts
`data/raw/hip2.json` from it, and `scripts/build-sky.mjs` and `build-stars.mjs` derive the
shipped files. The Hipparcos mission is ESA's.

## Deep-sky facts

The Messier objects' positions, magnitudes and sizes are d3-celestial's `messier.json` (BSD-3,
above). Distances, position angles, common names and the 30 other showpieces (the Magellanic
Clouds, ω Centauri, 47 Tucanae, the Carina Nebula, the Double Cluster, …) are catalogue facts
hand-entered and rounded in `scripts/build-dso.mjs` from the NGC/IC, SEDS Messier pages, NED and
Harris (2010) for globular clusters. No OpenNGC (CC BY-SA) data is used.

## GeoNames — Creative Commons Attribution 4.0

`data/cities.json` contains data from **GeoNames** (<https://www.geonames.org/>), licensed
under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Sources: `cities15000.zip`,
`countryInfo.txt`, `admin1CodesASCII.txt` and `timeZones.txt` from
<https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/>.

Changes made by `scripts/build-cities.mjs`: a subset of about 1,600 places (capitals, cities of
500,000 or more, the three largest per country and the largest per time zone); coordinates
rounded to 4 decimals; first-level admin and country names joined in; and the time zone of
places that GeoNames assigns to another country's zone (for example northern Vietnam →
`Asia/Bangkok`) replaced by their own country's single, identical zone (`Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh`).

## JPL Horizons data

`data/craft.json` is derived from heliocentric state vectors computed by the JPL Horizons system
(NASA/JPL-Caltech, <https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/>): Voyager 1 (-31), Voyager 2 (-32), New
Horizons (-98) and the James Webb Space Telescope (-170), thinned to adaptive Hermite knots by
`scripts/build-craft.mjs`. The orbital elements of Halley's Comet in `js/astro/comets.js` are those
Horizons serves for its per-apparition solutions (DASTCOM records 90000018, 90000023, 90000029 and
osculating elements integrated from 90000030); the solutions for the old apparitions go back to
D. K. Yeomans and T. Kiang, "The long-term motion of comet Halley", MNRAS 197, 633 (1981). JPL's
data are produced by a NASA laboratory and are not subject to copyright in the United States; they
are credited here as JPL requests.

## Historical records

The guest-star dates, peak magnitudes and remnant identifications in `js/astro/guest-stars.js`, and
the captions of the Moments in `js/core/moments.js`, are facts taken from the published sources
each entry cites (chiefly F. R. Stephenson and D. A. Green, *Historical Supernovae and their
Remnants*, Oxford University Press, 2002). No text or tables are reproduced.
